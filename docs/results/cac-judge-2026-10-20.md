# FordCast: CAC judge and product audit

Review date: October 20, 2026 (America/Chicago). Mandate: the attached “Brutal Congressional App Challenge Judge & Principal Product/UX Auditor” prompt, with particular weight on functionality, UI and community impact.

**Verdict: the current app demonstrates a potentially useful commute workflow, but its most consequential promises are inconsistent across screens. It is not ready to be presented as a dependable flood-avoidance navigation service. More features will not fix that. Make the existing journey trustworthy and understandable first.**

### Judge’s two-minute video verdict

**Strongest wow moment:** the Memorial Day moving car approaching a named crossing, then the red abandoned segment and blue detour appearing. This converts an abstract flood signal into an understandable decision. It is a staged illustration using archived signals and a precomputed road trip, not proof that a road would have flooded or that the detour was higher ground.

**Single biggest weakness:** the app presents experimental gauge evidence as actionable road-arrival knowledge while different surfaces disagree about the actual hazard. A skeptical judge can move from an official closure on the map to a HUD that ignores it. That failure is more damaging than an imperfect animation.

The honest impact story today is: **“Know which low-water crossings are near your commute, see official warnings and available creek evidence, and understand when to avoid travel.”** The claim “upstream sensors give drivers 15–60 minutes before road flooding” has not been established by this dataset. The current proxy study has 4 hits, 8 misses and 1 false alarm; its 65-minute median describes detected downstream gauge-category events, not road flooding, and the nearest-gauge comparator has the same median with no false alarms. [Current study](C:/2025AppChallenge/docs/results/results.md).

## Section B: Top 10 critical issues

P0 means a blocker to presenting this build as dependable public driving-safety guidance. P1 means a major functionality/credibility defect. P2 means polish or secondary scope that should follow the core repairs.

### 1. P0 — Drive drops official closures and weather hazards

**Where:** [Drive route selection](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/drive.tsx:61), [combined hazard construction](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/monitor.tsx:405), [danger combination](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/danger.ts:49).

**Evidence — Traced:** Drive filters `s.monitored`, takes six nearby records, then copies `s.state` and `s.window`. The monitor stores combined closure/NWS/rain results in `danger`, `reasons` and `displayState`. Consequently an unmonitored officially closed crossing is excluded, and a calibrated crossing can retain `no_rise` in the HUD even when its combined state is closed. This is not active-route sequencing; it is a nearby sensor subset.

**Why it hurts:** the screen explicitly marketed for hands-free use can omit the most authoritative warning. UI polish does not compensate for the wrong warning reaching the driver.

**Concrete fix:** create one route-ahead hazard selector shared by Home, Drive, notifications and Guardian. Include official-only crossings. Rank combined reasons and carry their source/freshness into the HUD. Without an active trip, label it “Nearby warnings.” Regression cases: calm sensor plus official closure, unmonitored closure, NWS warning, hazard behind driver, and route with more than six records.

### 2. P0 — Experimental gauge lags are described as road-arrival forecasts

**Where:** [consumer copy](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/locales/en.json:51), [sensor travel copy](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/locales/en.json:248), [hardcoded home metric](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/HomeScreen.tsx:438), [Storms metric](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/StormsScreen.tsx:102), [tier claims](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/components/ProvenanceCard.tsx:23).

**Evidence — Observed/Traced:** Home says “38 min / Median early warning”; Storms reports 65. Crossing detail says water usually takes 60–90 minutes from sensor to crossing. Drive says water could arrive in 0–15 minutes. These are gauge-to-gauge proxy parameters; road transfer and topology remain unverified. Provenance labels all 30 Tier-B records empirical gauge corridors, while only four records currently have a live calibrated link. Surveyed elevation alone would also not prove the `road_forecast_verified: true` claim shown in the hypothetical Tier-A description.

**Why it hurts:** the large consumer claim overrules a caveat buried in the science section. Counts of records, sensors and forecast coverage look interchangeable when they are not.

**Concrete fix:** bind every metric to the generated results with its scope; remove the 38-minute constant and fallback. Show “Experimental creek signal” rather than a road-arrival countdown until road validation exists. Name official closures directly. Separate mapped records, physical crossings, research candidates and calibrated links. Show the 4/12 detection result beside lead time, not many scrolls below it. Never infer road verification solely from a surveyed elevation.

### 3. P0 — Background protection can replace known warnings with a fresh empty cache

**Where:** [Guardian refresh](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/guardian.ts:37), [cache write](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/guardian.ts:60), [foreground cache timestamp](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/monitor.tsx:513).

**Evidence — Traced, not device-reproduced:** Guardian refresh calls only USGS and closures. It omits the NWS polygon hazards used in the foreground. `Promise.allSettled` allows both fetches to fail; refresh still writes `{at: now, hazards: []}` and resolves successfully, so the caller’s `.catch` does not preserve old hazards. Foreground cache updates stamp the current time without recording the observation age of each contributing source.

**Why it hurts:** “FordCast keeps watching” implies protection survives handoff, precisely when the background implementation can lose a known warning or omit an entire source.

**Concrete fix:** cache per-source success, fetched time, observation time, expiry and retained hazard IDs. Failure must not mean an all-clear. Carry NWS warnings into background evaluation and explicitly report degraded coverage. Use closure-specific retention rather than indefinitely trusting or deleting them. Test all-source failure, one-source failure, expired warning, app suspension and recovery on installed iOS/Android builds before promoting this feature.

### 4. P1 — Route checks do not verify the navigation path the user will actually take

**Where:** [hazard-only route planner](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/routing.ts:175), [three-waypoint handoff](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/routing.ts:232), [route card](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/home/RoutePanels.tsx:199), [extension reconstruction](C:/2025AppChallenge/extension/fordcast.js:246), [extension alternative](C:/2025AppChallenge/extension/fordcast.js:279).

**Evidence — Traced/Observed:** the app avoids entries currently classified as hazards; an unmonitored crossing without a flagged source is absent from that hazard list. The extension reconstructs a Valhalla route from Google URL endpoints/stops rather than checking Google’s actual displayed polyline. It tries one alternative and can retain flagged crossings; the CTA still says “Open safer route.” Three deviation waypoints cannot guarantee Google follows every checked segment. With location off the app computes from downtown, yet the result card does not name that origin.

**Why it hurts:** “Flood-checked route” sounds like coverage of the route being followed. An unknown crossing or a different Google path invalidates that interpretation.

**Concrete fix:** show explicit origin and let users edit it without GPS. Distinguish “reported hazards avoided” from “unmonitored crossings remain.” Recheck the final alternative against the full inventory and shared hazard policy. Disable navigation suggestions through remaining blocking hazards. The extension should say “Estimated route between these stops” until it can validate the displayed route. A handoff must show that continuous monitoring is unavailable on web; do not promise background protection there.

### 5. P1 — Commute audit miscounts the central value proposition

**Where:** [corridor scan and six-record truncation](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/home/RoutePanels.tsx:222), [summary counts](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/home/RoutePanels.tsx:248), [automatic-protection copy](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/locales/en.json:469).

**Evidence — Observed/Traced:** the Onion Creek Park audit calls three E. William Cannon device records plus one road record four crossings. `found.slice(0, 6)` is applied before counts/history totals are computed, so routes with more than six matches are silently undercounted. A 120m proximity test can also include a nearby parallel road; it is not proof the route crosses that waterway.

**Why it hurts:** sunny-day usefulness depends on explaining real exposure. Inflated duplicate counts and hidden truncation undermine the feature that gives people a reason to keep the app.

**Concrete fix:** maintain physical-crossing groups with linked inventory/device IDs; do not merge solely by distance where separate roads exist. Count all groups before limiting the rendered preview; show “6 of N.” Label proximity honestly until road/stream intersection matching is verified. Count distinct NOAA events across nearby groups. Example: “1 crossing area on this route; 4 source records.”

### 6. P1 — Live detail silently switches to an archived chart; arrival buckets disagree

**Where:** [live/archive choice](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/app/crossing/[id].tsx:115), [science hydrograph](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/app/crossing/[id].tsx:307), [arrival definitions](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/components/ArrivalBucketStrip.tsx:31), [risk contract](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/risk.ts:11).

**Evidence — Observed/Traced:** live detail showed −0.20ft; expanded chart accessibility described upstream 4.13ft and downstream 5.67ft from a replay. The code supplies an archived replay and its hero time even in live mode. The seven visual buckets differ from the seven allowed model windows: for example valid `[30,60]`, `[0,30]` and `[15,60]` cannot exactly match the strip’s keys and lose their active highlight. The strip instead advertises ranges out to 180 minutes that the model contract does not emit.

**Why it hurts:** two believable graphs on one page can tell different temporal stories. A window that vanishes from the visual explanation damages both comprehension and scientific credibility.

**Concrete fix:** show live charts only from the live snapshot. Put historical evidence behind an explicit dated mode transition with a persistent banner. Generate every arrival view from one shared window contract; test all seven valid windows and null. Use human explanations rather than field names. Make selected storm/crossing/reference/time visible on every research chart.

### 7. P1 — Live requests and source freshness lack a coherent failure policy

**Where:** [refresh requests](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/monitor.tsx:230), [route computation](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/monitor.tsx:525), [USGS parsing](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/live.ts:55), [NWS parsing](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/hazards.ts:104), [sensor explorer](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/home/RoutePanels.tsx:581).

**Evidence — Traced:** APIs accept abort signals but the monitor and route planner do not create bounded request lifetimes. Repeated manual/interval refreshes can overlap and late responses can overwrite newer data. Empty USGS features count as a successful fetch. Source `ok` flags do not expire with time; NWS expiration is parsed but not checked in point matching. Sensor cards read cached last values without applying the live rule’s staleness gate. Route UI warns for USGS/ATX failures but not equivalent NWS/rain gaps. Creek flood levels come from bundled `nws_categories`, not the separately vetted reference cache.

**Why it hurts:** a successful HTTP response is not a fresh or complete safety check. “Live · just now” can refer to fetching old observations. A stalled request can leave route planning stuck.

**Concrete fix:** add shared timeout/cancellation, generation IDs and source-specific validation/backoff. Store request success separately from fresh observation coverage. Render “Updated now; latest reading 41m old.” Distinguish empty-but-valid source results from malformed payloads. Expire weather warnings and mark unlocated warning geometry as incomplete coverage. Use the same freshness and vetted-category logic on map markers, sensor cards, detail and routes. Tests should cover 429, hanging request, empty/malformed response, stale readings and late-response races.

### 8. P1 — Voice is split across engines, and drill audio loses its safety context

**Where:** [speech cleaning](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/voice.ts:106), [keyword clip selection](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/voice.ts:132), [playback fallback](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/lib/voice.ts:236), [Drive speech](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/app/(tabs)/drive.tsx:124), [clip generation](C:/2025AppChallenge/tools/build_neural_voice.py:65).

**Evidence — Traced:** `cleanForSpeech` removes DRILL/SIMULACRO. Trip clips omit that prefix and claim a “higher-ground detour” without elevation checking. Road-name matching can substitute a fixed demo clip for a dynamic live phrase. An old `audio.play().catch` can start fallback speech after stop/mute because there is no cancellation token. Drive uses Expo Speech and its own `muted` state, bypassing the selected persona and monitor voice preference. The 195-character streaming fallback can truncate the action at the end of a message. Browser persona clicks worked; sound quality and actual output were not verified.

**Why it hurts:** the most polished voice can say the wrong thing, resume after mute, or sound like a live emergency during a demonstration.

**Concrete fix:** one audio controller with a session ID, global mute, persona and language, source mode and priority. Preserve “Simulation” in spoken drills. Use explicit clip keys only for the exact corresponding message; never infer a live hazard from a road name. Gate all async fallback by the active session. Put action first and avoid unsupported “higher ground.” Verify mute during playback, mute during rejected playback, route changes, app backgrounding and screen navigation on device.

### 9. P1 — Mobile hides the useful first action and splits one trip into two sessions

**Where:** [225px sheet](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/HomeScreen.tsx:630), [demo starts in peek](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/HomeScreen.tsx:669), [car run state](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/features/demo/useTripDemo.ts:33), [arrival progression](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/features/demo/useTripDemo.ts:135).

**Evidence — Observed/Traced:** 390px home shows map, sensor numbers, navigation, then a “no watched crossings” message cut by the sheet. The commute/demo actions sit below it. Demo Stop and the reason for rerouting are not reliably visible in peek. The alert banner overlays the live/drill controls. Full sheet covers the mode toggle while the brand remains visible. Switching to Drive starts a separate session. The car hook has no focus/AppState pause. A `no_way` phase still advances the animation toward arrival because only `arrived` stops its clock.

**Why it hurts:** the first-time user sees no useful local result; the judge must hunt for the wow moment; the demo can visually drive into a route it says cannot be taken.

**Concrete fix:** put “Check a trip” and “Try a 45-second storm example” above the fold. Use a short sticky route/demo summary with Stop/Pause always visible. Reserve space for warnings instead of covering mode controls. Share a trip session between Map and Drive; define explicit transition rules for tab changes and backgrounding. A no-route state must stop before the hazard and say wait. Include persistent “SIMULATION” on every related surface.

### 10. P2 — The force “simulator” adds unsupported precision and distracts from the decision

**Where:** [fixed force table](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/components/fc/Kit.tsx:424), [12-inch claims](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/locales/en.json:490), [repeated placement in Storms](C:/2025AppChallenge/mobile/src/screens/StormsScreen.tsx:198).

**Evidence — Observed/Traced:** depth buttons switch among hardcoded uplift/side-force pairs: 750/280, 1500/560 and 3000/1120 pounds. There is no vehicle geometry, flow speed, drag coefficient, displacement model or calculation exposed. Depth alone does not establish those forces. It is an illustration, not a scientific simulator.

**Why it hurts:** exact-looking numbers invite a skeptic to dismantle a secondary feature. Repeating it in Alerts and Storms lengthens both screens without making the next driving decision clearer.

**Concrete fix:** remove the force readouts and call it a brief flood-safety illustration, linked to the NWS guidance. If keeping a model, document inputs/equations/units and uncertainty and never depict a safe drive-through threshold. NWS supports the danger of entering floodwater; it does not support this app’s vehicle-specific force table. [NWS guidance](https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-turn-around-dont-drown).

## Section C: Screen-by-screen UI/UX tear-down

### Map / Home

- **Remove/simplify:** the three vanity metrics before the main action. Keep regional inventory size in an About/coverage panel. Put one next action and a clear local condition summary in the sheet.
- **Current → better:** “No FordCast-watched crossings within 3 mi” → “Showing downtown Austin. Set a starting point to check your trip.” A user with GPS off must not mistake default-location distances for personal ones.
- **Current → better:** “Watched (30)” → “Research candidates (30)”; separately show the actual calibrated-link count. “Master Standpipe/Slave Arm” → a physical road-and-creek name with device records in secondary source details.
- **Layout:** mobile screenshot shows a good portion of the screen occupied by map labels before any meaningful action is visible. Header tagline truncates. Full sheet hides mode controls. Standard browser scrollbars appear as bright gutters against the dark sheet.
- **Map interaction:** short demo zoom/pan test worked; user pan relinquished follow mode. Keep that behavior. Background dots have small visual targets and do not communicate whether a crossing is merely mapped or actively checked. Use a larger invisible hit area and a direct accessible name for every marker, including car/destination.
- **Colour:** warning red is prominent; dim blue dots and low-contrast street labels carry little meaning on the first screen. Do not increase saturation everywhere. Prioritize route and imminent named warning; fade unrelated gauges until selected.

### Route & Commute Audit

- **Remove/simplify:** duplicate Remove route controls, two save-place chips, share, a caution note and a test-hazard control compete with navigation. Put testing tools in a demo/debug section. Keep origin, destination, current warnings, coverage gaps and primary action first.
- **Current → better:** “Flood-checked route” → “Route checked for reported hazards.” “Checked against 2052 crossings in region” → “Route corridor: N physical crossing areas; X with creek evidence; Y unmonitored.”
- **Current → better:** “Commute Flood Exposure Audit” → “Crossings on your trip.” “During heavy rain, FordCast monitors and routes around them automatically” → a conditional statement naming coverage and platform limits.
- **Layout:** at 1366px the roughly 430px sidebar works as a comparison surface, but the audit sits below the handoff controls and long disclaimer. Move the route-specific crossing summary above Google handoff. On 390px, reveal the route panel to at least half-height when planning starts and preserve map space for the selected hazard.
- **Interaction:** empty search, aborted search, stale suggestions, changed origin and concurrent destination requests need explicit states. Latest-request-wins is currently missing from route computation. Do not leave the last successful route looking current after a failed replan.

### Creeks

- **Remove/simplify:** a flat list of 19 technical station names. Default to sensors relevant to the route/selected crossing, with an expandable regional list.
- **Current → better:** “1.03 ft” → “Gauge height 1.03ft · measured 12m ago.” State explicitly that height is measured relative to the station datum, not water depth on the road. Negative gauge heights are not necessarily invalid, but without this explanation they look broken.
- **Current → better:** “Flood level: 5.0 ft” → “Published river flood stage: 5.0ft · source.” Verify each bundled category before showing it; a map category is not a road threshold.
- **Layout:** full mobile sheet clipped station names to “Barton Creek at Loop 3…” while showing several nearly identical rows. Preserve the distinguishing road name, move agency boilerplate to detail, and show stale/missing state in text beside the value. Do not rely on blue colour for health.

### Storm Drill & Car Demo

- **Remove/simplify:** crossing-directory content from the active demonstration. Show the car, upcoming crossing, why the warning fired, and the decision. A scrollable regional directory distracts from the causal story.
- **Current → better:** “Rerouted around Henry Marx … +9 min” → “Simulation: route avoids the flagged crossing · +9 min.” “Water may already be on the road” → experimental gauge language unless official road evidence is supplied.
- **Interaction:** maintain free zoom/pan; keep a visible Resume follow control. Current animation reacts to danger, but the initial storm time is deliberately aligned to the decision point and the detour is precomputed. Explain that staging; do not call it a historical reconstruction of a real driver being saved.
- **Layout:** keep Pause/Stop and simulation timestamp in a fixed strip; reserve a banner slot. The current red banner obscures the top mode control and the sheet can show unrelated downtown crossings while the demo car is elsewhere.
- **Failure paths:** no-way-around must hold the car short of the crossing. Switching Live should end the simulation; moving to Drive should continue the same session or explicitly stop it, rather than create an unrelated one.

### Storms & Physics Simulator

- **Remove/simplify:** five equivalent cards each with three actions, plus impact numbers, force readouts, a backtest paragraph and eight repetitive hotspot records. Lead with one recommended example; put the rest in a compact selector.
- **Current → better:** “Run one as a drill to see exactly what FordCast would have told you” → “Replay archived observations under today’s experimental rule.” This is retrospective modeling, not an audited historical service log.
- **Current → better:** “Median early warning lead” → “Gauge-reference lead among detected events.” Place missed events and false alarms beside the number. Group duplicated hotspot device records and preserve “within 2km” in the list, not just detail.
- **Interaction:** the clicked 6/12-inch illustration changed, but it does not calculate forces. Drop the pseudo-precision; one short, sourced safety lesson is enough. Put the honest study comparison before optional education content.

### Drive HUD

- **Remove/simplify:** the quoted paragraph and Previous/Next browse controls. Driving should focus on one action, one location and one source; repeat/mute/end are sufficient in motion.
- **Current → better:** “Water could arrive in 0–15 min” → the current authoritative reason, e.g. “Officially closed — take another route,” or “Experimental creek signal — road conditions unverified.” Avoid showing incompatible certainty such as a precise road-arrival range and an unverified-road caveat elsewhere.
- **Layout:** the 390px warning is large enough to notice, but the icon/title card and a second arrival-warning card repeat urgency and push controls down. Make source and trip mode readable without a paragraph. Rename the start control to distinguish a real active route from a nearby-warning view.
- **Functionality:** use combined hazards, forward route order and the global audio controller. The present HUD is not `CockpitScreen.tsx`; the active route file implements a separate view. Repair the active component, not the similarly named legacy science cockpit.

### Alerts & Voice

- **Remove/simplify:** route integration instructions, recent logs, saved crossings, force simulation and safety education from a single long settings page. Show a compact readiness checklist; keep logs and education in disclosures.
- **Current → better:** “Get a notification and a spoken warning when a crossing near you starts to flood” → “Choose alerts for official closures and available creek signals.” There is no verified onset detector for road flooding.
- **Current → better:** “Select neural voice persona” → “Warning voice.” Technical voice branding is not the user’s task. Show Preview/Stop preview and whether playback succeeded; the current test produced no visible playback status.
- **Permissions:** current off-state text is clear enough to locate controls. Permission prompts and notification delivery were not tested. Distinguish web foreground-only monitoring from an installed background-capable build before sending the user to another app.
- **Copy:** “iPhone silent” belongs only on supported iOS behavior. English persona descriptions, hardcoded share summaries and developer fields require a full Spanish pass; toggling the locale does not establish translation quality.

### Crossing Detail

- **Remove/simplify:** six similarly weighted action buttons before evidence. Keep status/reason/source age, then one primary “View route” action. Put share/save/report/external sources into secondary controls.
- **Current → better:** “Show science details (for judges)” → “How this signal is estimated.” “Truth provenance passport & evidence tier ladder” → “Evidence and limitations.” Keep `calibration_kind`, `h_u` and τ in optional technical documentation.
- **Current → better:** “Water usually takes…” → “Gauge-to-gauge lag estimate; travel to this road is unverified.” The nearby NOAA narrative caveat is useful, but truncated event prose needs an expand control and direct event source.
- **Layout/interaction:** two hydrographs must not silently represent different dates. Source, event date and live/replay mode should sit above each plot. Use identical arrival windows everywhere. The full evidence tier ladder repeats project architecture rather than answering whether this particular crossing has validated evidence.

### Google Maps Extension

- **Remove/simplify:** automatic floating-panel takeover of the map viewport. Provide a compact route-status chip with an expandable explanation and a user-controlled position, then verify it does not cover Google controls.
- **Current → better:** “Your route” → “Estimated route between your stops” until matching to the displayed Google route is established. “Safer route” → “Alternative avoiding these reported hazards.” If `still.length > 0`, do not keep a reassuring CTA label.
- **Functionality:** source reconstruction, one-shot alternative selection, duplicate data/rule implementations and three-waypoint limits need parity tests against the mobile policy. The pure test suite does not test extension lifecycle, a real Google route change, multiple candidate routes, panel focus, CSP/runtime messaging or accessibility.
- **Distribution:** “install the extension folder in this project” is a developer instruction, not an ordinary commuter onboarding flow. Keep it labeled experimental until a supported distribution and real Chrome/Edge journey are verified. No extension was installed during this review.

## Section D: High-impact upgrades worth building

These are focused upgrades to the existing product, not invitations to add more independent modules. None implies City of Austin/TxDOT readiness without operational and field validation.

| Upgrade | Concrete experience | Why it matters | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. A trustworthy “Check my trip” home** | Above-fold origin + destination, one route summary, grouped physical crossing areas, source freshness and unknown coverage. Saved Home/Work becomes a one-tap repeat check. | Gives a normal commuter a reason to open the app on a dry day. | New users can identify the origin, crossing exposure and next action in 10 seconds; audit totals agree with reviewed physical groups; “N total / 6 shown” tested on long routes. |
| **2. One trip, one warning, everywhere** | Start on Map, continue the same trip in Drive, hear the same sourced warning, end it once. Web explicitly identifies foreground-only limits. | Makes functionality feel dependable and reduces contradictory advice. | Shared fixtures produce identical official-closure/NWS/unknown states in map, HUD, voice, route and background paths; mute/stop remain effective across navigation and rejected audio playback. |
| **3. A 45-second evidence-first storm story** | One named road trip, dated simulated data, a clear before/after route decision, then the measured result including misses and false alarms. Pause/Stop always visible. | Preserves the strongest visual moment while letting a skeptical judge verify the claim. | No unsupported higher-ground/road-onset claim; all five storm paths including false alarm and no-route cases work; camera remains controllable at 390px and desktop. |
| **4. Source-health and coverage on the route itself** | A plain-language strip: closures checked at X, creek readings Y old, weather unavailable, N crossings unmonitored. On failure it explains what changed and which warnings are retained. | Converts engineering resilience into a user-visible reason to trust or limit the result. | Automated timeout/429/empty/stale/recovery tests; installed-device background tests; no failure can create a fresh all-clear. |
| **5. A shareable crossing-area preparedness card** | One physical location, official source links, nearby historical context labeled by area/date, and “What to do when this road is closed.” Bilingual and accessible. | Provides practical community value without pretending to predict an unvalidated road depth. | Source links resolve; duplicate nearby NOAA events are not multiplied; comprehension review with local commuters and Spanish speakers; no personal trip or location shared without deliberate user action. |

### Implementation order

1. Unify the combined hazard and trip state; repair Drive and background failure behavior.
2. Remove unsupported road-arrival/coverage claims, live/archive mixing and contradictory time windows.
3. Make the commute flow the home-screen primary action; fix physical-crossing counts and origin clarity.
4. Consolidate audio and harden route requests/handoff; verify actual extension behavior.
5. Polish the demonstration and optional education after those contracts hold.

Do not add another dashboard or simulator before these are done. The missing product advantage is a completed, credible decision loop: **my trip → crossing ahead → current evidence → appropriate action → consistent warning**.
